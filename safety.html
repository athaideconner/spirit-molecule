Harm reduction

First aid for a hard trip

A calm, practical card for sitters and friends. Print it. Keep it. This is not a guide to taking drugs — it is how to reduce harm if someone is already in distress.

Jump to red flags

Read this first

Nothing on this page is medical advice or a recommendation to use psychedelics. If someone may be having a medical emergency, call local emergency services immediately. In the US call/text 988 for mental-health crisis support; in the UK & ROI call 116 123.

For the sober sitter

What to do, step by step

Your job is safety and calm presence — not to “fix” the experience or argue with it.

01

Stay calm yourself

Speak slowly and softly. Your nervous system is contagious. Sit at their level; avoid hovering or panicking.

02

Check the basics

Are they breathing normally? Any injury risk (standing near stairs, water, traffic)? Move hazards away. Keep them from driving or wandering alone.

03

Reduce stimulation

Dim lights, lower music, fewer people in the room, quieter voices. Overstimulation often fuels fear loops.

04

Reassure without debating

Useful lines: “You're safe. I'm here. This will pass.” Avoid arguing about what they're seeing. Don't force them to “snap out of it.”

05

Ground gently

Offer a blanket, a hand to hold if they want it, slow breathing together, or naming simple sensory facts (“Your feet are on the floor”). Let them refuse touch.

06

Wait it out when you can

Smoked DMT peaks are often short (minutes). Longer compounds need more patience. Stay present until they are clearly reoriented.

07

Aftercare

Water, quiet, warmth, no pressure to “process” immediately. Check in the next day. Integration can take time — shame and isolation make it harder.

Get help now

Medical red flags

Call emergency services if you see any of these — do not wait for the trip to “wear off.”

  • Chest pain, severe shortness of breath, or collapse
  • Seizure, unconsciousness, or inability to wake/respond
  • Very high fever, extreme rigidity, or confusion with agitation that looks medical (possible serotonin toxicity)
  • Known dangerous combination with MAOIs/SSRIs/stimulants and rapidly worsening symptoms
  • Suicidal intent with means, or clear danger to self/others
  • Suspected overdose of an unknown or adulterated substance

Be honest with responders

Tell emergency services what was taken if you know — it helps them treat safely. You are seeking medical care, not a debate about legality in the moment of crisis.

Quick reference

Do / Don't

Do

  • Keep a sober person present
  • Use a calm voice and short sentences
  • Offer water if they can drink safely
  • Protect privacy and dignity
  • Leave when you're out of your depth — call pros

Don't

  • Don't leave them alone if distressed
  • Don't pile on more substances “to fix it”
  • Don't shame, film, or mock them
  • Don't restrain unless needed to prevent injury
  • Don't ignore red-flag physical symptoms

Presence is the intervention. Calm is contagious.
