Glossary

Key terms

DMT (N,N-Dimethyltryptamine)

A powerful, short-acting psychedelic compound found in many plants and animals. Structurally related to serotonin; acts mainly via the 5-HT2A receptor.

Ayahuasca

A traditional Amazonian brew that combines a DMT-containing plant with an MAO-inhibiting vine, making DMT orally active for several hours in a ceremonial context.

5-MeO-DMT

A different chemical from DMT (not the same molecule). Often more potent, less visual, and associated with intense ego dissolution. Requires even greater caution.

Breakthrough

A common term for a high-intensity DMT experience in which ordinary reality seems fully replaced by an immersive visionary state.

Ego dissolution / ego death

The temporary loss of the usual sense of a separate “I.” Can feel liberating or terrifying depending on set, setting, and support.

Set and setting

Set is your mindset, intention, and mental state. Setting is your physical and social environment. Together they strongly shape the experience.

Integration

The work after an experience: reflecting, journaling, talking, and slowly applying any insights to ordinary life. Often considered the real medicine.

MAO / MAOI

Monoamine oxidase is an enzyme that breaks down DMT in the gut. MAO inhibitors (as in ayahuasca) block that enzyme — and can be dangerous with many medications and some foods.

Serotonin syndrome

A potentially life-threatening reaction from too much serotonin activity — a known risk when combining MAOIs (or ayahuasca) with SSRIs, SNRIs, and other drugs.

Default mode network (DMN)

A brain network linked to self-referential thinking and the stable sense of self. Psychedelics often reduce its usual integrity, which tracks with ego dissolution.

Dieta

A traditional Amazonian practice of restricted food, sex and social contact — often in isolation — while taking “teacher plants,” commonly paired with ayahuasca ceremonies at retreats.

Icaros

Healing songs sung by Amazonian healers, such as Shipibo onanyabo, to guide an ayahuasca ceremony.

Entheogen

A substance used in a spiritual or sacred context to occasion experiences of the divine or the profound — emphasizing intention, not recreation.

Harm reduction

Practical strategies that reduce risk and injury even when abstinence isn't chosen — screening, sober sitters, clear information, and crisis support.

FAQ

Common questions

Is this site recommending that I use DMT?

No. This is educational and harm-reduction information only. It is not medical, legal, or psychological advice, and it does not encourage illegal activity.

Is DMT addictive?

Classic serotonergic psychedelics like DMT are generally considered to have low physical dependence potential compared with opioids, alcohol, or stimulants. That does not make them safe or risk-free.

How long do effects last?

Vaporized/smoked DMT is typically intense for minutes and largely over within about 5–20 minutes. Ayahuasca usually lasts 4–6 hours because of MAO inhibition.

Is DMT made in the human body?

Trace endogenous DMT has been detected in humans, but its natural role is still unknown. Claims about pineal “floods” at birth or death are hypotheses, not established facts. See the science page.

Who should avoid psychedelics?

People with personal or family histories of psychosis, schizophrenia, or bipolar disorder; people with significant cardiovascular disease; and anyone on interacting medications (especially MAOIs/SSRIs with ayahuasca) face elevated risk. When in doubt, talk to a qualified clinician — and know that “no” is a valid answer.

What's the difference between DMT and 5-MeO-DMT?

They are different molecules. 5-MeO-DMT is often more potent, less visual, and associated with total ego dissolution. It has a narrow dosing window and serious adverse-event risk. See the comparison page.

What should I do if someone is having a hard trip?

Stay calm, keep them safe and hydrated if appropriate, reduce stimulation, reassure without arguing about the content of the experience, and get emergency help for medical red flags. Full steps: hard-trip first aid.

Is DMT legal where I live?

Usually no — DMT is controlled in most countries. Some places have religious exemptions for ayahuasca or local decriminalization for certain natural psychedelics. Use the country lookup, then verify with an official source. This is not legal advice.

Can psychedelics treat depression?

Early clinical research — especially with psilocybin, plus smaller ayahuasca trials and, in 2026, the first placebo-controlled trial of DMT itself — shows promise in controlled settings with screening and support. Promising is not proven for unsupervised use. See the evidence.

Are ayahuasca retreats safe?

Some are run carefully; many are not, and almost none are licensed or inspected. Participants have died, been sexually assaulted, or been robbed at retreats, and in 2025 the U.S. Embassy in Peru and Costa Rica's Ministry of Health issued public warnings. If you're considering one, use the 12-question vetting checklist and talk to your own doctor first — especially if you take any medication.

Is there a legal way to get DMT-based therapy?

Narrowly. Clinical trials of DMT and ayahuasca are the main medically supervised route; ayahuasca churches have church-specific religious exemptions in some countries; and Colorado may consider adding DMT to its regulated program after June 2026. See legal routes.

Where can I get crisis help?

US: call or text 988. UK & ROI: Samaritans 116 123. If someone is in immediate danger, call local emergency services. For non-emergency psychedelic peer support in the US, the Fireside Project takes calls and texts at 62-FIRESIDE (623-473-7433).
