The story

A timeline of DMT

Long before chemists gave it a name, DMT was woven into the ritual life of the Americas.

~1,000+ years ago · Sacred origins
Ritual snuffs & ayahuasca in the Americas

Indigenous peoples across the Amazon and Caribbean used DMT-containing snuffs (yopo/cohoba, from Anadenanthera seeds) and the ayahuasca brew ceremonially. Archaeologists have found ancient ritual kits that tested positive for DMT and related compounds — evidence of a very long tradition.

1931 · First synthesis
DMT made in a lab

Canadian chemist Richard Manske first synthesized DMT — but its psychoactivity went unnoticed at the time.

1956 · Psychoactivity discovered
Szára's self-experiments

Hungarian chemist Stephen Szára, unable to obtain LSD during the Cold War, synthesized and tested DMT on himself — documenting its intense, short-lived psychedelic effects.

1960s · Counterculture
“The businessman's trip”

DMT entered Western psychedelic culture, later championed and mythologised by figures like Terence McKenna, whose vivid accounts shaped its reputation.

1970–1971 · Prohibition
Scheduled and banned

The U.S. Controlled Substances Act (1970) placed DMT in Schedule I, and the UN Convention on Psychotropic Substances (1971) made that prohibition global. Research effectively froze for two decades.

1990s · Research reawakens
Strassman's DMT trials

Rick Strassman ran the first U.S.-approved human psychedelic studies in a generation, reopening the door to legitimate DMT science. See the science.

2006 onward · Exemptions & reform
Religion, then decriminalization

Courts began carving out religious exemptions for ceremonial ayahuasca, and from 2019 a wave of U.S. cities and states started decriminalizing or regulating natural psychedelics — a still-unfolding shift.

2025–2026 · Warnings and evidence
Retreat alarms, and the first DMT trial win

In January 2025 the U.S. Embassy in Peru and Costa Rica's Ministry of Health warned the public about unregulated ayahuasca retreats after deaths and assaults. In 2026 the first placebo-controlled trial of DMT for depression was published — science and the law moving on very different tracks. See healing retreats.

Where the law stands

Legality around the world

Laws change — verify before you trust this

This is a general, simplified snapshot for education, not legal advice, and drug law is changing fast. Rules differ by country, state, and city, and penalties can be severe. Always confirm the current law in your own jurisdiction from an official source.

US

United States

Federally Schedule I — illegal to manufacture, possess or distribute. However, some cities (e.g., Oakland, Santa Cruz) have decriminalized entheogenic plants, and Colorado's 2022 Natural Medicine Health Act decriminalized personal use (not sale) of several natural psychedelics, including DMT. Colorado's licensed healing centers opened with psilocybin only; the law lets the state consider adding DMT after June 1, 2026, so check Colorado's Department of Regulatory Agencies for the current status. Local reality varies widely.

RF

Religious exemptions (U.S. & Canada)

The U.S. Supreme Court's 2006 Gonzales v. O Centro Espírita Beneficente União do Vegetal ruling protected a church's sacramental ayahuasca use under religious-freedom law; the Santo Daime church later won similar protections. In Canada, Health Canada has granted case-by-case exemptions to a few Santo Daime and UDV congregations since 2017. These are narrow, church-specific exemptions — not a general legal pathway.

SA

Amazonian & South America

Ayahuasca has protected or regulated status in parts of its homeland. Peru recognizes it as cultural heritage; Brazil permits religious ceremonial use. Traditional/ceremonial contexts are treated very differently from recreational use — and neither country licenses the retreat centers that sell ceremonies to tourists.

EU

UK & much of Europe

In the UK, DMT is a Class A drug — among the most serious classifications. Most of Europe prohibits it, though enforcement and ayahuasca-ceremony tolerance vary by country (e.g., historically more permissive case law in the Netherlands, now tightened).

CA

Canada & Australia

Both prohibit DMT outside authorized research/medical exemptions. Notably, Australia rescheduled psilocybin and MDMA for prescribed medical use in 2023 — but that reform does not cover DMT.

UN

The global baseline

Under the 1971 UN Convention, DMT is an internationally controlled substance in most of the world. The clear default assumption anywhere should be: illegal unless specifically proven otherwise.

How to read these labels

Illegal means controlled with no general personal-use pathway. Mixed means local decriminalization, religious exemptions, or gray areas — still high risk. Traditional means recognized ceremonial/cultural frameworks (often ayahuasca), not open recreational legality.

The shifting map

A moment of change

After 50 years of blanket prohibition, the legal landscape is moving for the first time in a generation — unevenly, and mostly for other psychedelics first.

  • Decriminalization ≠ legalization. Decriminalizing usually means lower enforcement priority or reduced penalties — not that a substance is legal to buy, sell or use freely.
  • Most reform targets psilocybin first. Oregon and several jurisdictions built regulated psilocybin-therapy systems; DMT is usually further behind, if included at all.
  • Religious and Indigenous rights are a distinct legal track from medical or recreational reform, and often the only protected route for ayahuasca.
  • Medical research access is expanding even where personal use remains illegal — the two move on separate timelines.
  • Retreat tourism sits in the gaps. Most ayahuasca retreats operate in places where the brew is tolerated or culturally protected, but the centers themselves are rarely licensed or inspected. How to vet one.

Why the law and the science disagree

DMT was scheduled in the 1970s largely for political and cultural reasons, before modern research existed. Today's studies suggest low dependence potential and real therapeutic promise — creating a growing tension between the legal classification and the emerging evidence that reform movements are now trying to resolve.

An ancient sacrament, a modern prohibition, and a future still being written.
